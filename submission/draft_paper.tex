\begin{abstract}
Fact-verification systems can expose evidence while leaving the decisions that
connect them to a verdict opaque. \system{} is an interactive demonstration in
which reviewers inspect claim decomposition, retrieved source sentences, joint
evidence assessments, and source-bound symbolic checks. They can correct
evidence relations and observe deterministic recomputation in a reasoning graph.
The model is restricted to server-issued evidence and premise identifiers,
while Python validates grounding, executes eligible checks, and composes the
four-way verdict.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Real-world verification requires more than a label: a reviewer must be able to
locate a missed passage, an over-read contradiction, or an invalid inference.
Recent demonstrations support claim-level factuality analysis across selectable
sources, evidence-grounded citation inspection, and trace-to-graph analysis
\cite{huang-etal-2026-ufo,lee-etal-2026-reflens,wu-etal-2026-agentgraph}.
\system{} instead brings the full source-bound path from a supplied claim and
documents to an inspectable, user-correctable verdict into one workflow.

AVeriTeC supplies evidence and justifications for real-world claims
\cite{schlichtkrull-etal-2023-averitec}; WiCE and FactLens study fine-grained
verification through subclaims \cite{kamoi-etal-2023-wice,mitra-etal-2025-factlens},
whose decomposition can itself introduce downstream errors
\cite{wanner-etal-2024-closer,hu-etal-2025-decomposition}. ProgramFC separates
reasoning programs from execution functions \cite{pan-etal-2023-fact}, ProoFVer
makes a generated proof determine the verdict \cite{krishna-etal-2022-proofver},
and CHECKWHY connects evidence through argument structures
\cite{si-etal-2024-checkwhy}. \system{} contributes an audience-facing
integration of source-linked inspection, conservative execution, editable
evidence relations, and deterministic composition. It claims neither a new
general logic nor improved accuracy; exact span grounding detects provenance
errors, not semantic equivalence.

\section{System Design and Implementation}

Given a claim and supplied documents, \system{} decomposes the claim, retrieves
evidence, assesses evidence bundles, executes eligible source-bound checks,
aggregates accepted relations, and exposes the result for inspection. Reference
labels are display-only and never enter this computation.

\paragraph{Constrained decomposition and retrieval.}
Schema-constrained Qwen2.5~\cite{qwen-team-2024-qwen25} produces at most 12
atomic claims under a flat \textsc{Single}, \textsc{And}, or \textsc{Or}
composition. Each atom carries a contiguous claim locator, which the backend
converts to browser-compatible UTF-16 offsets and the interface verifies.
Invalid output receives one repair attempt; a second failure retains the whole
claim as one flagged atom.

PySBD segments documents into exact-offset sentences
\cite{sadvilkar-neumann-2020-pysbd}. BGE-small-en-v1.5
\cite{xiao-etal-2024-cpack} encodes the instructed query and uninstructed
passages. Both embeddings are $L_2$-normalized, so their dot product equals
cosine similarity. Equal-weight reciprocal-rank fusion is
\begin{equation}
h_{ij}=\frac{1}{60+r^d_{ij}}+\frac{1}{60+r^\ell_{ij}},
\label{eq:retrieval}
\end{equation}
following RRF \cite{cormack-etal-2009-rrf}. The lexical score combines exact
term coverage, exact numeric-string matches, and a list-entry bonus. Retrieval
ranks globally, then applies a budget-dependent per-document diversity limit;
the default is $k=6$. Cue-triggered reading context may add one adjacent
sentence on each side without making it an independently selectable anchor.

\paragraph{Grounded assessment and validation.}
A deterministic jurisdiction gate marks a candidate as mismatched only when
geography constrains the claim's scope, both the atom and evidence resolve to
non-empty country-code sets, and those sets are disjoint. Geography is exempt
when it is itself the claimed value, as in ``located in'' or ``member of.''
Mismatches remain visible but are withheld from Qwen assessment and symbolic
execution. Qwen assigns up to three candidate IDs per relation and labels the
joint bundle as sufficient, partial, or insufficient. Only support and
refutation from a sufficient bundle become verdict-bearing edges; the other
selections remain visible as provisional evidence.
Python extracts typed operator candidates and their permitted premise IDs. Qwen
may select only a server-issued candidate and a subset of those premises; it
cannot introduce a premise or return the answer. Python validates exact
grounding and operator constraints before execution, leaving invalid mappings
unresolved.

\paragraph{Deterministic execution and composition.}
Version 1 deliberately limits execution to exact list membership; Decimal
comparison of counts, percentages, and unconverted currency symbols;
absolute-date intervals; explicit-attribute patterns; two-field counting over a
fixed Nobel-field vocabulary; and extremum counterexamples. Absence supports
non-membership only when the cited span explicitly certifies a complete
enumeration and the item is absent from its extracted entries. One aligned
counterexample can refute an extremum, but the system otherwise abstains. Dates
denote closed intervals: $A$ is certainly before $B$ only if
$\max A<\min B$.

For each atom, the presence of accepted neural or Python-resolved support and
refutation edges determines a supported-only, refuted-only, conflicting, or
unresolved state. Conflicting means that the same atom has both support and
refutation; support for one conjunct and refutation of another instead refutes
the conjunction. For \textsc{And}, refuted-only takes precedence, then
conflicting, then unanimous supported-only; for \textsc{Or}, supported-only
takes precedence, then conflicting, then unanimous refuted-only. All other cases
are \textsc{NEI}. This is a documented decision policy, not standard four-valued
semantics. A model-produced material-omission flag overrides the result to
\textsc{Conflicting} only when it cites accepted support and available context.

\section{User Interface and Visualizations}

The recorded demonstration opens the claim ``Australia is a permanent member
of the UN Security Council.'' A reviewer selects its atomic claim, inspects the
authoritative member list, and follows the complete-list premise into the
membership check. The graph exposes the resulting refutation and final verdict.
The reviewer can then relabel an evidence candidate and observe deterministic
recomputation without rerunning the model (Figure~\ref{fig:interface}).

The interface presents parallel atomic-claim lanes, source evidence, bundle or
symbolic nodes, atom results, optional composition, and the final verdict.
Reading context is collapsed beneath its evidence anchor. Only sufficient model
relations and resolved checks appear in the default graph; provisional evidence,
scope mismatches, and unresolved checks remain inspectable. A collapsed spaCy
view highlights linguistic structure but remains verdict-neutral.

An 18-case walkthrough exercises the implemented workflow across decomposition,
retrieval, scope, symbolic checks, and verdict composition. It comprises 15
authored claims with two source excerpts each and AVeriTeC development cases 142,
146, and 392. Science, History, Geography, Technology, Current Affairs, and
AVeriTeC each contain three cases; the reference verdicts comprise seven
supported, seven refuted, two insufficient, and two conflicting examples.
Every constructed passage is contiguous and sentence-complete, and the interface
links to its full publisher page. The
presentation audit fixes expected atoms, operator outcomes,
premise grounding, verdicts, and a nine-node default-graph ceiling. Live
inference runs locally; the hosted site replays immutable schema-version-6 runs.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

\system{} demonstrates how source-linked model judgments, constrained checks,
and deterministic composition can be exposed as one editable evidence-to-verdict
trace. Its value is inspectability: each decisive relation can be traced to the
displayed source text and the mechanism that accepted it.

The system verifies claims only against supplied text; it neither establishes
source authority nor performs open-web retrieval. Exact locators do not ensure
semantic faithfulness, assessment and omission remain model judgments, and the
symbolic operators are narrow heuristics rather than general theorem proving.
Future work will first test whether reviewers can localize and correct seeded
retrieval, scope, and inference errors more effectively than with a
label-and-evidence baseline. We will also evaluate decomposition and verdict
quality on held-out claims and extend the narrow checks with broader typed
parsers and unit handling.
